# Meridian Marine Co. — First-Client Acquisition & Sales Execution System v1

## Objective

Turn the approved MMC offer into a repeatable process for finding, qualifying, converting, and onboarding the first recurring vessel clients.

## Sales funnel

**PROSPECT → CONTACTED → ENGAGED → DISCOVERY → QUALIFIED → PROPOSAL → DECISION → WON/LOST → ONBOARDING**

## Ideal first-client profile

Prioritize owners who:

- Own or operate a vessel in Cayman
- Spend meaningful time away from the vessel
- Value professional oversight
- Have a clear concern about visibility, maintenance, security, weather, vendors, or readiness
- Can understand the value of recurring oversight
- Fit the approved service scope and package structure

## Prospect sources

Use a balanced mix of:

- Existing personal/business network
- Marine/yacht professionals
- Marinas and dock relationships
- Referrals
- Local business relationships
- Direct prospecting

## Discovery conversation

The purpose is to understand the owner's situation, not force a package.

Explore:

- How often they are away
- Who currently checks the vessel
- What happens when something goes wrong
- How they receive updates
- Current vendor/management relationships
- Recent problems or near-misses
- What creates the most uncertainty
- What a better oversight experience would look like

## Qualification gate

A prospect is qualified when there is:

**CLEAR NEED + SERVICE FIT + REASONABLE ACCESS + WILLINGNESS TO PAY + APPROPRIATE EXPECTATIONS**

If those conditions are absent, do not force a sale.

## Proposal structure

A proposal should make the decision simple:

1. Owner's current concern
2. MMC's role
3. Recommended service level from the existing package baseline
4. What is included
5. What is excluded
6. Communication/reporting expectations
7. Coordination boundaries
8. Start/onboarding process
9. Price and payment terms using the approved commercial baseline

## Follow-up rhythm

After a proposal:

- Confirm receipt
- Answer questions
- Identify decision blockers
- Follow up at an agreed time
- Close the loop whether won or lost

Do not use pressure tactics.

## Objection handling

### "I already have someone checking the boat."

Understand what they currently receive. Position MMC around independent documentation, continuity, escalation, and follow-through where there is a genuine gap.

### "My marina handles it."

Clarify the difference between marina operations and owner-focused independent oversight.

### "I'll just ask a friend."

Acknowledge the convenience, then explain the difference in consistency, evidence, reporting, accountability, and accumulated vessel history.

### "I don't think I need it."

Do not argue. Determine whether there is actually a problem MMC solves.

## Close

The close should confirm:

- Service selected
- Vessel details
- Start timing
- Access
- Communication preferences
- Required approvals
- Onboarding next step

## Lost opportunity record

Record why qualified prospects did not convert, using categories such as:

- Price
- Timing
- No perceived need
- Existing provider
- Access/logistics
- Scope mismatch
- Decision delayed
- Competitor
- Other verified reason

## Sales KPIs

Track:

- New prospects
- Contact rate
- Discovery rate
- Qualification rate
- Proposal rate
- Close rate
- Sales cycle
- Acquisition source
- Recurring revenue won
- Lost-reason distribution

## First-client priority

The first clients should optimize for learning quality and service fit, not vanity volume.

Prefer customers who will:

- Give useful feedback
- Fit the operating model
- Allow MMC to learn real workload
- Provide opportunities for documented proof
- Potentially become strong referral sources

## Principle

Early sales are not merely about getting customers. They are how MMC tests its value proposition, service economics, delivery process, and ideal customer profile in the real world.
