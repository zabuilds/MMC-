# Meridian Marine Co. — First Client Pilot Program v1

## Objective

Use the first 1–3 real vessels to prove the MMC service, capture real operating data, and refine the system before scaling.

## Pilot structure

### Vessel 1 — Proof

Primary goal: prove MMC can deliver the complete promised experience reliably.

Focus:
- Onboarding
- Baseline
- Visit execution
- Evidence
- Findings
- Communication
- Reporting
- Follow-up
- Closure

### Vessels 2–3 — Repeatability

Primary goal: determine whether the workflow works consistently across different owners, vessels, and circumstances.

Focus:
- Time/capacity measurement
- Package fit
- Communication patterns
- Vendor coordination
- Exception frequency
- Client satisfaction
- Reporting effort

## Pilot rules

- Do not over-customize the service for one client without recording the reason.
- Record actual delivery time and operating costs.
- Document every material exception.
- Capture client feedback after meaningful service events.
- Preserve the approved scope and boundaries.
- Fix repeatable problems in the system rather than relying on individual memory.

## Pilot success criteria

The pilot is successful when MMC can demonstrate:

- Consistent service delivery
- Reliable Critical/Urgent escalation
- Clear evidence-backed reporting
- Controlled vendor coordination
- Acceptable response times
- Manageable operator workload
- Positive client confidence/trust
- Measurable economics
- A repeatable process that does not depend on improvisation

## Pilot review

After each pilot vessel, review:

**WHAT WORKED → WHAT FAILED → WHAT TO CHANGE → WHAT TO STANDARDIZE → WHAT NOT TO REPEAT**

## Graduation gate

Before actively scaling customer acquisition, confirm that the pilot has produced a stable service model, reliable SOPs, measured capacity assumptions, and clear package economics.

## Principle

The first clients are not merely revenue. They are the controlled proving ground for the MMC operating system.
