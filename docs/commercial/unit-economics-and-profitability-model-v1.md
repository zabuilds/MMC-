# Meridian Marine Co. — Unit Economics & Profitability Model v1

## Objective

Measure the real economic contribution of each recurring vessel relationship without inventing operating assumptions before actual delivery data exists.

## Package economics

### Harbour — CI$225/month

Track actual monthly delivery burden against the recurring fee.

### Steward — CI$395/month

Measure the economics of the hero package separately because its service promise and likely workload differ from Harbour.

### Reserve — CI$675/month

Measure the higher-touch service against actual recurring delivery burden, including readiness and exception workload.

### Custom

Model individually based on the agreed service scope.

## Cost categories

Track:

- Field visit time
- Travel time and travel cost
- Documentation/reporting time
- Follow-up time
- Vendor coordination time
- Communication/admin time
- Direct operating supplies
- Contractor/vendor costs attributable to MMC service delivery
- Payment/transaction costs once billing is active
- Allocated operating overhead

## Core metrics

**Monthly Revenue**

Recurring package revenue attributable to the vessel.

**Direct Service Cost**

Costs directly associated with delivering the promised service.

**Contribution**

Revenue minus direct service cost.

**Contribution Margin**

Contribution divided by revenue.

**Capacity Consumption**

Measured operating time consumed by the vessel relationship.

## Pricing rule

Do not change package pricing simply because a package appears expensive or inexpensive in isolation. Pricing should be reviewed against:

**CUSTOMER VALUE + SERVICE PROMISE + ACTUAL CAPACITY CONSUMPTION + DIRECT COST + TARGET CONTRIBUTION**

## Data-first approach

During the initial customer cohort, record actual time and cost data before locking detailed profitability assumptions.

## Management view

Review economics by:

- Package
- Vessel
- Client
- Acquisition source
- Service complexity
- Operator
- Month

## Warning signals

Flag relationships where:

- Service time materially exceeds expectations
- Travel burden is unusually high
- Vendor coordination becomes excessive
- Exception frequency is high
- Contribution falls below the approved operating threshold

## Decision framework

When economics are weak, evaluate in this order:

1. Improve operating efficiency
2. Clarify scope/boundaries
3. Adjust service configuration
4. Consider package migration
5. Consider custom pricing for genuinely complex requirements

Avoid automatic discounting as the first response.
