# Meridian Marine Co. — First 100 Vessel Growth & Capacity Model v1

## Objective

Grow recurring vessel oversight without allowing service quality to degrade.

## Growth stages

### Stage 1 — 1 to 10 vessels

Founder-led delivery and direct customer learning.

Priorities:
- Prove service consistency
- Refine visit standards
- Learn package fit
- Establish real service-time data
- Build referral momentum

### Stage 2 — 11 to 25 vessels

Introduce repeatable operating routines and begin separating field work from management/coordination work.

Priorities:
- Standardize scheduling
- Improve vendor network depth
- Measure operator capacity
- Protect report turnaround
- Identify first delegation points

### Stage 3 — 26 to 50 vessels

Move from founder-dependent operations toward a small operating team.

Priorities:
- Dedicated field capacity
- Management oversight
- Consistent QA
- Strong client communication
- Backup coverage

### Stage 4 — 51 to 100 vessels

Operate as a scalable service company rather than a founder-led practice.

Priorities:
- Team-based vessel coverage
- Capacity planning
- Management reporting
- Vendor network redundancy
- Service quality monitoring
- Customer retention

## Capacity principle

Never sell more recurring oversight than the operating team can reliably deliver.

Capacity must account for:

**VISIT TIME + TRAVEL + DOCUMENTATION + REPORTING + FOLLOW-UP + VENDOR COORDINATION + EXCEPTIONS**

## Package capacity logic

Harbour, Steward, and Reserve should consume different amounts of operating capacity according to their actual promised service levels. Capacity planning must be based on measured delivery time rather than package price alone.

## Trigger points

Review staffing/capacity whenever:

- Visit backlog begins increasing
- Report turnaround begins slipping
- Critical findings remain open longer than the service standard
- Operator workload becomes consistently excessive
- Vendor coordination consumes disproportionate management time
- Client communication quality begins declining

## Revenue planning

Track each stage using:

- Active vessels
- Package mix
- Monthly recurring revenue
- Average revenue per vessel
- Direct service cost
- Contribution per vessel
- Operating capacity utilization

## Growth rule

Revenue growth is only healthy when service quality, response time, client trust, and operational control remain within the promised standard.

## First-100 objective

Reach 100 vessels through controlled learning and capacity expansion, not aggressive sales that create an operational service failure.
