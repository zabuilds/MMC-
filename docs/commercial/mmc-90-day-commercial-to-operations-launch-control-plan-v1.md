# Meridian Marine Co. — 90-Day Commercial-to-Operations Launch Control Plan v1

## Purpose

Turn MMC's approved operating model into a controlled first-90-day launch sequence, while keeping legal implementation outside this workstream as requested.

## Launch principle

The objective is not maximum early volume.

The objective is to prove that MMC can repeatedly:

**SELL → ONBOARD → SERVE → DOCUMENT → REPORT → COORDINATE → VERIFY → COLLECT → RETAIN**

## Days 1–30 — Prove

### Commercial

- Build and work the initial prospect list.
- Conduct discovery conversations.
- Test positioning and service explanation.
- Identify objections and refine messaging.
- Establish first qualified opportunities.

### Operations

- Prepare the approved service checklist and visit workflow.
- Establish vessel baseline process.
- Establish evidence/reporting workflow.
- Establish finding/action escalation process.
- Run early service events with high founder oversight.

### Management

Track:

- Prospects
- Qualified opportunities
- New clients
- Active vessels
- Visits completed
- Findings/actions
- QA results
- Capacity status
- Cash/collections

### Gate

Do not accelerate simply because sales activity is high. Confirm the service can be delivered consistently.

## Days 31–60 — Repeat

### Commercial

- Continue prospecting through the validated channels.
- Build referral/partner activity.
- Improve conversion based on real objections.
- Monitor retention signals from early clients.

### Operations

- Standardize recurring work.
- Introduce controlled delegation where readiness criteria are met.
- Increase QA sampling.
- Improve vendor coordination.
- Track recurring defects and workflow bottlenecks.

### Management

Review:

- Package workload versus assumptions
- Contribution/economics
- Operator/founder capacity
- QA pass rate
- Client health
- Open/overdue actions
- Vendor performance
- Cash position

### Gate

MMC should demonstrate repeatability before materially increasing operational complexity.

## Days 61–90 — Controlled Scale

### Commercial

- Increase acquisition activity only within available service capacity.
- Strengthen referral channels.
- Improve conversion and retention processes.
- Identify the strongest customer segments and acquisition sources.

### Operations

- Expand delegation where proven.
- Increase capacity only when justified by workload and economics.
- Strengthen QA and exception management.
- Reduce founder involvement in routine work.
- Preserve client experience while volume increases.

### Management

Review the command KPIs:

**RECURRING REVENUE + CASH + ACTIVE VESSELS + VISIT COMPLETION + URGENT/CRITICAL ITEMS + QA PASS RATE + CAPACITY + OVERDUE ACTIONS**

### Gate

Move into the next growth stage only if:

- Service quality remains controlled.
- Capacity is not persistently Red.
- Cash position supports the next stage.
- Client relationships remain healthy.
- Core workflows are repeatable.
- Founder dependency is declining where appropriate.

## Weekly operating rhythm

### Monday

**DEMAND → SCHEDULE → CAPACITY → PRIORITIES**

### During the week

**SERVE → DOCUMENT → ESCALATE → COORDINATE → REPORT**

### Friday

**QA → OPEN ACTIONS → CLIENT HEALTH → CASH → CAPACITY → NEXT WEEK**

## Launch scorecard

At minimum, management should know:

- How many prospects are active?
- How many qualified opportunities exist?
- How many paying clients are active?
- How many vessels are under service?
- Are visits being completed on time?
- What important findings/actions remain open?
- Are reports being delivered to standard?
- Is QA holding?
- Is capacity Green, Yellow, or Red?
- Are collections occurring as expected?
- Is cash protected?
- What is the single biggest constraint?
- What decision is required next?

## Commercial-to-operations handoff

A client should not become operationally active until the required commercial and service information is complete.

**QUALIFIED → AGREED SERVICE → ONBOARDING COMPLETE → VESSEL BASELINE → SERVICE READY → FIRST VISIT**

## First-client discipline

Early clients are evidence-generation opportunities as well as revenue relationships.

Use them to learn:

- Actual service workload
- Real client expectations
- Reporting preferences
- Common findings
- Vendor needs
- Capacity consumption
- Package economics
- Retention drivers

Learning should improve the system without casually changing the approved core architecture.

## Pause triggers

Management should consider slowing acquisition or expansion when:

- QA materially deteriorates
- Critical findings are not being handled reliably
- Overdue actions accumulate
- Capacity remains Red
- Founder workload becomes unsustainable
- Cash buffer falls below the approved threshold
- Client-impacting failures become recurring

## Principle

The first 90 days are a controlled proof period. MMC earns the right to scale by demonstrating repeatable service quality, healthy economics, reliable operations, and client trust.
