# Meridian Marine Co. — Financial Controls, Unit Economics & Cash Management System v1

## Purpose

Define how MMC connects service delivery to revenue, direct costs, contribution, collections, cash, and controlled growth.

## Financial model

The core economic chain is:

**PACKAGE → SERVICE WORK → DIRECT COST → CONTRIBUTION → OVERHEAD → OPERATING RESULT → CASH**

## Revenue control

Revenue should be traceable to an approved commercial agreement and service plan.

Do not create informal pricing or undocumented recurring commitments.

Every active client relationship should have a clear commercial basis.

## Unit economics

Evaluate each service package using:

- Monthly revenue
- Expected visit frequency
- Average visit duration
- Travel/access burden
- Reporting/admin time
- Vendor coordination burden
- Expected direct labor/capacity cost
- Other direct service costs
- Contribution

The objective is to understand whether each package produces enough contribution to justify the operational capacity it consumes.

## Capacity-to-economics link

A vessel is not simply a revenue unit. It consumes capacity.

Management should evaluate:

**REVENUE PER VESSEL → WORKLOAD → DIRECT COST → CONTRIBUTION → CAPACITY CONSUMED**

This prevents growth decisions based solely on top-line revenue.

## Cash management

Cash should be monitored separately from accounting profit.

Core controls include:

- Cash balance
- Expected collections
- Upcoming committed expenses
- Recurring operating expenses
- Vendor obligations
- Tax/reserve obligations where applicable
- Minimum operating cash threshold

## Collections

The intended collection flow is:

**BILL → DUE → COLLECTION → RECONCILE**

Overdue amounts should be visible and assigned for follow-up.

## Expense controls

Expenses should have:

- Business purpose
- Appropriate authorization
- Supporting record/receipt where required
- Correct category
- Reconciliation status

Material commitments should not be made without appropriate authority.

## Vendor financial separation

Third-party vendor charges must remain distinguishable from MMC service revenue and costs.

Where owner-funded vendor work is coordinated, the underlying vendor commitment and client authorization should remain traceable.

## Financial decision thresholds

Management should define thresholds for:

- Routine operating spend
- Material vendor commitments
- Emergency spend
- Discounts/credits
- Refunds
- Pricing changes
- New recurring commitments

Thresholds should be documented before they are needed.

## Cash protection rule

Growth should not continue merely because demand exists.

Before committing to additional fixed costs or material workforce expansion, evaluate:

**DEMAND → REVENUE → COLLECTION TIMING → DIRECT COST → FIXED COST → CASH BUFFER → DECISION**

## Package economics review

Package performance should be reviewed periodically against actual operating data.

If actual workload materially exceeds assumptions, management should investigate:

- Scope creep
- Underpricing
- Inefficient processes
- Excessive travel burden
- Vendor coordination load
- Reporting/admin overhead

Possible responses include process improvement, scope clarification, pricing review, or capacity changes.

## Management financial dashboard

The management view should eventually show:

**RECURRING REVENUE + COLLECTIONS + CASH + DIRECT COST + CONTRIBUTION + OPERATING EXPENSE + CAPACITY**

This should support weekly operating decisions rather than simply retrospective accounting.

## Financial controls and billing architecture

The approved Stripe/billing architecture remains authoritative.

This document does not create new pricing, duplicate billing state machines, or replace the approved payment design.

## Auditability

Material financial events should preserve:

- Source/client
- Amount
- Date
- Authorization
- Status
- Related service/billing record
- Reconciliation outcome

## Principle

MMC should grow from healthy economics and controlled cash—not from revenue volume alone. Every new client, vessel, package, vendor commitment, and staffing decision should be understandable in terms of workload, contribution, and cash impact.
