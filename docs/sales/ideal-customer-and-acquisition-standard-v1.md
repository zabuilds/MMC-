# Meridian Marine Co. — Ideal Customer & Acquisition Standard v1

## Primary customer

The ideal MMC customer is a boat or yacht owner who:

- Owns a valuable vessel in Cayman or regularly keeps one in Cayman.
- Is absent from the vessel for meaningful periods.
- Wants independent oversight rather than full yacht management.
- Values prevention, documentation, responsiveness, and trusted coordination.
- Has enough asset value and complexity that a recurring oversight service is economically sensible.
- Prefers a single trusted point of coordination instead of managing multiple vendors personally.

## Priority segments

### 1. Absentee owners
Highest priority. Owners who live elsewhere and need confidence that their vessel is being watched between visits.

### 2. Frequent but time-constrained local owners
Owners who live in or visit Cayman but do not want to personally coordinate recurring vessel care.

### 3. High-value recreational vessels
Owners whose asset value, complexity, or replacement cost makes proactive oversight especially valuable.

### 4. Captains, brokers, marinas, and marine professionals as referral partners
These are strategic channels rather than the primary customer.

## Acquisition channels

Priority order for launch:

1. Trusted referrals from existing boat owners.
2. Yacht/boat brokers and sales professionals.
3. Captains and crew.
4. Marinas and marine service businesses.
5. Insurance and related professional relationships where appropriate.
6. Direct outreach to qualified vessel owners.
7. Local premium-network relationships and events.

## Positioning rule

MMC should sell **peace of mind through independent oversight**, not maintenance labor.

The strongest commercial message is the owner's outcome: knowing someone trustworthy is paying attention when the owner is not there.

## Qualification signals

Strong prospects typically have at least three of:

- Vessel absent from owner for extended periods.
- High-value or complex vessel.
- Multiple service/vendor relationships.
- Frequent travel.
- Previous maintenance surprises.
- Frustration with unreliable contractors or informal checks.
- Need for pre-arrival readiness.
- Desire for documented condition reports.

## Disqualification signals

MMC should be cautious with prospects seeking:

- Cheap cleaning/detailing only.
- Unlimited captain/crew availability.
- Full yacht management at an unrealistic budget.
- Unlicensed repair work.
- Constant ad-hoc labor without defined scope.
- Services that create unacceptable safety, legal, or insurance exposure.
