# Meridian Marine Co. — First 25-Vessel Growth & Capacity Stage-Gate System v1

## Objective

Move MMC from initial clients toward 25 active vessels without allowing sales growth to outrun service quality, capacity, cash, or management control.

## Stage 1 — Vessels 1–5

Primary objective: prove the service.

Focus:

- Founder-led delivery
- Validate client fit
- Validate package economics
- Capture real workload
- Refine SOPs
- Establish reporting quality
- Identify recurring operational friction

### Gate to Stage 2

Proceed when:

**SERVICE IS RELIABLE + CLIENTS SHOW VALUE + WORKLOAD IS UNDERSTOOD + MAJOR DEFECTS ARE CONTROLLED**

## Stage 2 — Vessels 6–10

Primary objective: prove repeatability.

Focus:

- Standardize recurring workflows
- Strengthen vendor network
- Improve scheduling
- Measure capacity
- Begin structured delegation
- Increase QA sampling
- Identify strongest acquisition channels

### Gate to Stage 3

Proceed when:

**REPEATABLE DELIVERY + ACCEPTABLE ECONOMICS + CONTROLLED CAPACITY + DOCUMENTED OPERATING SYSTEM**

## Stage 3 — Vessels 11–15

Primary objective: prove controlled scaling.

Focus:

- Delegate proven repeatable work
- Formalize operator training
- Strengthen management reporting
- Monitor client health
- Reduce founder dependency
- Protect service quality during growth

### Gate to Stage 4

Proceed when:

**DELEGATION WORKS + QA REMAINS STRONG + FOUNDER BOTTLENECKS DECLINE + RECURRING REVENUE SUPPORTS CAPACITY**

## Stage 4 — Vessels 16–20

Primary objective: build operating leverage.

Focus:

- Expand qualified capacity
- Develop redundancy in critical functions
- Improve workflow efficiency
- Strengthen partner/referral channels
- Monitor margin by complexity
- Formalize management cadence

### Gate to Stage 5

Proceed when:

**CAPACITY IS PREDICTABLE + QUALITY IS STABLE + ECONOMICS ARE UNDERSTOOD + MANAGEMENT CONTROL IS STRONG**

## Stage 5 — Vessels 21–25

Primary objective: prepare for the next operating scale.

Focus:

- Review organizational structure
- Review operator capacity
- Review vendor concentration
- Review client concentration
- Review package economics
- Review retention/referral performance
- Identify technology/process bottlenecks
- Establish next-stage growth requirements

## Universal growth guardrails

MMC should not add volume simply because demand exists if:

- Visit completion is slipping
- Reports are late
- QA defects are rising
- Critical issues are being missed
- Client communication is deteriorating
- Open actions are accumulating
- Cash cannot support the added capacity
- Founder workload is becoming unsustainable

## Capacity rule

Growth is permitted when additional capacity is available **before** service quality becomes constrained—not after failure appears.

## Founder dependency metric

Track the percentage of core operating work that requires direct founder involvement.

The target is progressive reduction without reducing quality or accountability.

## Growth dashboard

At minimum monitor:

- Active vessels
- Recurring revenue
- Visits due/completed
- Report turnaround
- Open/overdue actions
- Critical/urgent incidents
- QA defects
- Client health
- Vendor exceptions
- Capacity utilization
- Contribution margin
- Founder workload

## Stage-gate principle

Every growth stage must answer:

**CAN WE SELL IT? → CAN WE DELIVER IT? → CAN WE DELIVER IT CONSISTENTLY? → CAN WE AFFORD THE CAPACITY? → CAN WE CONTROL THE BUSINESS AT THE NEXT SIZE?**

## Principle

MMC should scale through controlled stage gates, not arbitrary vessel-count targets. Twenty-five vessels is a milestone, not permission to compromise the service promise.
