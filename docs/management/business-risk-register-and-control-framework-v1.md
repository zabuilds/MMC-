# Meridian Marine Co. — Business Risk Register & Control Framework v1

## Objective

Identify the risks that could materially damage MMC's clients, service quality, cash position, reputation, or ability to operate, and assign practical controls before those risks become incidents.

## Risk categories

### Client risk

- Misunderstood expectations
- Poor communication
- Loss of trust
- Sensitive information mishandling
- Service failure

### Operational risk

- Missed visit
- Incomplete inspection/oversight scope
- Poor evidence
- Missed escalation
- Unclosed action
- Operator error

### Vendor risk

- Vendor non-performance
- Delayed response
- Poor workmanship by third parties
- Vendor concentration
- Conflicts of interest

### Financial risk

- Slow collections
- Insufficient operating cash
- Underpriced packages
- Uncontrolled expenses
- Growth ahead of cash capacity

### People risk

- Founder dependency
- Operator unavailability
- Training gaps
- Capacity overload
- Poor performance

### Technology risk

- System outage
- Data loss
- Unauthorized access
- Automation failure
- Incorrect or stale records

### External risk

- Severe weather
- Marina/access disruption
- Transportation disruption
- Market changes
- Major supplier disruption

### Growth risk

- Adding vessels faster than capacity
- Declining service quality during growth
- Excessive fixed overhead
- Poor-fit customers
- Over-expansion before economics are proven

## Risk rating

Rate each material risk using:

**LIKELIHOOD × IMPACT = RISK LEVEL**

Use:

- Low
- Moderate
- High
- Critical

## Control types

### Preventive

Designed to stop the risk from occurring.

Examples: training, access controls, checklists, scope definitions.

### Detective

Designed to identify problems quickly.

Examples: QA reviews, alerts, reconciliation, exception reporting.

### Corrective

Designed to limit damage and restore normal operations.

Examples: escalation, recovery procedures, vendor alternatives, corrective action.

## Core control framework

For every material risk define:

**RISK → OWNER → PREVENTIVE CONTROL → DETECTION → RESPONSE → RECOVERY → REVIEW**

## High-priority controls

MMC should maintain strong controls around:

- Critical/urgent escalation
- Client communications
- Vessel access information
- Evidence and reporting
- Financial commitments
- Sensitive client data
- Vendor relationships
- Operator authority
- System access
- Business continuity

## Risk ownership

Every material risk should have a clear accountable owner, even when execution is delegated.

Delegation does not eliminate accountability.

## Incident learning

When a material incident occurs:

**CONTAIN → DOCUMENT → INVESTIGATE → CORRECT → VERIFY → ROOT CAUSE → CONTROL UPDATE**

## Risk review cadence

### Weekly

Review active high-impact exceptions.

### Monthly

Review material risks, control failures, and emerging risks.

### Quarterly

Review the entire risk register and whether controls remain appropriate as MMC grows.

## Risk appetite

MMC should have low tolerance for risks involving:

- Client trust
- Safety-related escalation failures
- Confidentiality
- Material reporting inaccuracies
- Uncontrolled financial commitments

Greater tolerance may be appropriate for reversible operational inefficiencies that do not materially affect clients, safety, or financial stability.

## Growth gate

If a major risk becomes uncontrolled, management should be willing to pause the affected growth activity until an adequate control exists.

## Principle

Risk management is not about eliminating every possible problem. It is about knowing the important risks, controlling what can be controlled, detecting failures early, and responding deliberately when something goes wrong.
