# Meridian Marine Co. — Service Capacity Planning v1

## Purpose

Prevent MMC from selling more recurring service than it can reliably deliver.

## Capacity model

Track capacity across:

- Scheduled vessel visits
- Travel time
- Reporting time
- Vendor coordination
- Owner communication
- Emergency/priority interruptions
- Administrative work
- Weather/access delays

## Capacity principle

A package is operationally viable only when its recurring workload can be delivered reliably while preserving room for urgent issues and quality control.

## Early operating assumptions

Use actual first-client data to establish realistic averages for:

- Minutes per Harbour visit
- Minutes per Steward visit
- Minutes per Reserve visit
- Average travel burden
- Average report preparation time
- Average coordination time
- Frequency of unplanned work
- Frequency of emergency/priority interruptions

Do not lock permanent staffing ratios from assumptions alone.

## Capacity alerts

Management should review capacity when:

- Scheduled work repeatedly approaches available field capacity.
- Reports are being delayed.
- Owner communications are slipping.
- Open actions are accumulating.
- Emergency response would materially disrupt scheduled service.
- Travel time becomes disproportionately high.

## Scaling rule

Increase client volume only when service quality, response standards, and operational records remain reliable. When capacity tightens, adjust scheduling, geography, staffing, package boundaries, or client qualification before allowing quality to deteriorate.
