# Meridian Marine Co. — Vessel Inspection Standard v1

## Purpose

Create a repeatable, professional baseline for every MMC vessel visit while keeping observations within MMC's role as an independent oversight and coordination service.

## Visit sequence

1. Confirm vessel, access, and visit purpose.
2. Record arrival condition and obvious external conditions.
3. Walk exterior areas that are safely accessible.
4. Walk interior living and operational areas that are safely accessible.
5. Observe agreed visible systems and equipment for obvious abnormal conditions only.
6. Check for signs of water intrusion, leaks, unusual odors, corrosion, damage, pests, unsecured items, or environmental issues.
7. Verify agreed readiness/housekeeping indicators without performing specialist work.
8. Capture evidence for material or noteworthy observations.
9. Classify findings.
10. Escalate urgent/material issues according to package and authority.
11. Complete report and follow-up actions.

## Standard observation categories

### Exterior
- Hull and topside visible condition
- Decks and walkways
- Mooring/line condition visible from safe access
- Covers and visible fittings
- Visible storm/environmental damage
- Standing water or obvious drainage issues

### Interior
- Water intrusion indicators
- Unusual odors
- Visible mold/moisture indicators
- Electrical or equipment warning indicators visible without specialist testing
- Doors, hatches, and obvious access/security condition
- General environmental condition
- Signs of pests or intrusion

### Mechanical/equipment observations

MMC may visually observe agreed equipment and indicators for obvious abnormalities. MMC does not diagnose mechanical/electrical failures or represent observations as professional technical findings.

Examples:
- Unusual alarm indicators
- Obvious leaks
- Abnormal sounds or odors when safely observable
- Visible damage
- Apparent power/status indicators
- Equipment left in an unusual state compared with the owner's baseline

## Finding classification

### Critical
Immediate risk to people, vessel, security, or major property damage. Escalate immediately using the agreed emergency contact path.

### Urgent
A material issue that should be addressed promptly but does not appear to require immediate emergency intervention.

### Attention
A condition that should be tracked or coordinated but is not presently urgent.

### Monitor
A minor or unchanged observation appropriate for routine reporting.

## Evidence standard

Material findings should include, where practical:
- Date/time of observation
- Vessel/location reference
- Clear description
- Photo/video evidence when useful and safe
- Classification
- Recommended next coordination step
- Owner/vendor status when follow-up begins

## Safety rules

- Never enter an unsafe area to obtain evidence.
- Never operate unfamiliar machinery or systems solely to test them.
- Never bypass safety equipment or controls.
- Do not perform repairs unless separately authorized, qualified, and appropriately insured.
- Stop the visit when conditions present a credible safety risk.
- Escalate suspected emergencies rather than attempting specialist intervention.

## Package application

Harbour uses the standard inspection baseline with concise reporting.

Steward uses the same baseline with greater frequency, enhanced documentation, maintenance follow-up, and coordination.

Reserve uses the same baseline with weekly oversight, premium documentation, priority coordination, and expanded tracking.

The inspection standard remains consistent across packages; package differentiation comes primarily from frequency, responsiveness, coordination depth, reporting detail, and readiness support.
