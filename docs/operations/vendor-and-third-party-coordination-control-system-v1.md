# Meridian Marine Co. — Vendor & Third-Party Coordination Control System v1

## Purpose

Define how MMC coordinates trusted third parties while preserving owner trust, clear accountability, evidence, and the boundary between independent oversight and contractor work.

## MMC's role

MMC acts as:

**OWNER ADVOCATE + COORDINATOR + OVERSIGHT LAYER**

MMC does not represent itself as the technical contractor when a third party performs the actual work.

## Vendor lifecycle

**IDENTIFY → QUALIFY → APPROVE → ENGAGE → SCHEDULE → EXECUTE → DOCUMENT → VERIFY → CLOSE → REVIEW**

## Vendor qualification

Where appropriate, evaluate:

- Relevant capability
- Reliability
- Responsiveness
- Reputation/track record
- Insurance/licensing where relevant
- Service area
- Availability
- Pricing transparency
- Conflict-of-interest considerations

The required level of diligence should match the risk and nature of the work.

## Vendor relationship record

Maintain useful operational information such as:

- Vendor identity
- Service category
- Primary contacts
- Availability
- Vessel/service relevance
- Performance history
- Approved status
- Notes on capabilities and limitations

Do not treat vendor records as endorsements beyond what MMC has actually verified.

## Engagement workflow

When a client-approved action requires a vendor:

**ACTION → OWNER AUTHORIZATION IF REQUIRED → VENDOR SELECTION → REQUEST → RESPONSE → SCHEDULE → EXECUTION → EVIDENCE → VERIFICATION → CLIENT UPDATE**

## Authorization boundary

MMC must distinguish between:

- An observation
- A recommendation
- A request for quotation
- An owner's authorization
- A vendor's technical work
- MMC's verification of completion

MMC should not imply that owner approval exists when it does not.

## Vendor communication

Material vendor communication should be linked to the relevant client, vessel, finding, action, or incident.

Track:

- Request
- Date/time
- Contact
- Response
- Commitment
- Scheduled work
- Completion information
- Supporting evidence
- Verification outcome

## Verification

MMC's verification should confirm what can reasonably be verified within its scope.

Examples:

- Work was reported complete
- Vendor provided completion evidence
- Visible condition appears consistent with the completed work
- Required documentation was received

MMC should not certify specialist technical work beyond its competence or authority.

## Vendor failure

If a vendor misses a commitment:

**DETECT → ASSESS IMPACT → CONTACT VENDOR → ESCALATE/ALTERNATIVE → UPDATE CLIENT IF MATERIAL → TRACK → VERIFY**

## Critical vendor categories

For high-impact recurring categories, maintain alternatives where practical to reduce concentration risk.

## Conflicts of interest

Material conflicts or relationships that could reasonably affect independent oversight should be disclosed and managed according to MMC policy.

## Client choice

Where the owner has a preferred vendor, MMC should respect that choice while maintaining clear records of what MMC is coordinating versus what the vendor is responsible for.

## Pricing and invoices

Vendor pricing and invoices should remain distinguishable from MMC's own service charges.

Any pass-through or coordination-related commercial treatment must follow the approved MMC commercial architecture rather than creating ad hoc billing practices.

## Performance review

Track vendor performance using practical measures:

- Responsiveness
- Reliability
- Completion quality
- Timeliness
- Communication
- Documentation
- Repeat issues

Poor performance should influence future vendor selection.

## Risk controls

Additional controls apply to work involving:

- Safety
- High-value equipment
- Security
- Major repairs
- Emergency response
- Significant client expenditure

Higher-risk work may require additional authorization, documentation, or independent specialist verification.

## Principle

MMC creates value by controlling the coordination and oversight layer—not by pretending to be every contractor. The owner should always know who is responsible for the work, what MMC is responsible for, what was authorized, what happened, and what was verified.
