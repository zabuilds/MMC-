# Meridian Marine Co. — Business Continuity & Disruption Readiness v1

## Objective

Maintain client confidence and essential oversight when normal operations are disrupted.

## Disruption categories

- Severe weather or storm event
- Vessel access failure
- Marina/dock disruption
- Vendor failure
- Operator unavailability
- Founder unavailability
- Technology/system outage
- Communications outage
- Transportation disruption
- Unexpected critical vessel event

## Continuity priorities

When capacity is constrained, prioritize:

1. Safety and protection of people/property
2. Critical and urgent client/vessel matters
3. Scheduled oversight for higher-risk vessels/events
4. Time-sensitive vendor coordination
5. Routine administrative work

## Response sequence

**DETECT → ASSESS → PRIORITIZE → COMMUNICATE → ACT → DOCUMENT → RECOVER → REVIEW**

## Severe weather/event readiness

Before an anticipated event:

- Identify affected vessels
- Review access and marina conditions
- Review open vulnerabilities
- Confirm contact/escalation paths
- Prioritize pre-event checks where appropriate
- Communicate expected service changes

After the event:

- Reassess access and safety
- Perform priority checks when safe and authorized
- Document visible conditions
- Escalate critical findings immediately
- Provide owner updates
- Track recovery actions through closure

## Vendor failure

Maintain more than one viable option for critical categories where practical.

If a primary vendor fails:

**IDENTIFY FAILURE → ASSESS IMPACT → CONTACT ALTERNATIVE → INFORM CLIENT IF MATERIAL → TRACK → VERIFY**

## Operator unavailability

Routine work should be transferable through documented SOPs and trained backup capacity.

Critical matters require a clearly defined escalation owner rather than assuming the unavailable operator will respond.

## Founder unavailability

The business should maintain enough documentation, access, contact information, workflow clarity, and delegated authority that essential service does not depend on immediate founder availability.

## Technology outage

If systems become unavailable, use controlled temporary procedures for essential operations and reconcile records into the authoritative system after recovery.

Do not allow temporary records to become an uncontrolled parallel system.

## Communication continuity

If normal communication channels fail, use the approved backup communication method and document material communications when systems recover.

## Service degradation rule

If disruption prevents MMC from meeting a promised service level, communicate the limitation rather than silently pretending normal service occurred.

## Recovery

After disruption:

- Reconcile outstanding visits
- Reconcile open actions
- Confirm client communications
- Verify vendor commitments
- Restore records
- Review service defects
- Update SOPs where lessons reveal a weakness

## Continuity readiness review

Periodically verify:

- Backup operator capability
- Critical vendor alternatives
- Client contact information
- Vessel access information
- Escalation contacts
- Essential equipment
- Communication backups
- Record recovery procedures
- Founder-dependency risks

## Principle

MMC's continuity promise is not that disruption will never happen. It is that disruption will be recognized, prioritized, communicated, documented, and managed without abandoning the owner's interests.
