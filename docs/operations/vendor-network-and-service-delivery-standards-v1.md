# Meridian Marine Co. — Vendor Network & Service Delivery Standards v1

## Objective

Create a dependable vendor network while preserving MMC's role as independent oversight and coordination.

## Vendor qualification

Evaluate vendors on:

- Relevant capability
- Reputation
- Reliability
- Communication
- Response time
- Availability
- Insurance/credentials where appropriate
- Pricing transparency
- Quality of completed work
- Cayman service coverage

## Vendor categories

Maintain coverage across relevant marine needs, including:

- Mechanical
- Electrical
- Plumbing
- HVAC/refrigeration
- Detailing/cleaning
- Fiberglass/structural
- Electronics/navigation
- Engines/generators
- Haul-out/yard services
- Fuel/service
- Emergency response
- Other vessel-specific specialties

## Vendor assignment

Use the following flow:

**FINDING → SCOPE → APPROVAL IF REQUIRED → VENDOR MATCH → ASSIGN → ACKNOWLEDGE → SCHEDULE → COMPLETE → EVIDENCE → VERIFY → CLOSE**

## Vendor communication standard

Every assignment should clearly state:

- Vessel
- Location
- Requested work
- Relevant evidence
- Required timing
- Access requirements
- Client approval status where applicable
- MMC contact
- Expected completion/confirmation

## Independence boundary

MMC coordinates and verifies service delivery but does not misrepresent itself as the technical contractor performing specialist work.

## Vendor performance scorecard

Track:

- Response time
- Completion time
- Communication quality
- Scope adherence
- Evidence/confirmation quality
- Rework/callback frequency
- Client impact
- Reliability

## Vendor escalation

If a vendor misses a commitment:

**FOLLOW UP → ESCALATE → REASSIGN IF NEEDED → INFORM CLIENT WHEN MATERIAL → VERIFY ALTERNATIVE → DOCUMENT**

## Vendor redundancy

For critical service categories, develop more than one reliable option where practical.

Do not allow a single vendor to become an invisible single point of failure.

## Vendor relationship management

Maintain professional relationships without allowing vendor relationships to compromise MMC's independence or client-first responsibility.

## Client approval

Where work, cost, or scope requires client authorization, MMC must obtain the required approval before committing the client to that expenditure or expanded scope.

## Quality control

Vendor completion does not automatically equal MMC closure.

MMC closes the operational item only after the required evidence and verification steps are satisfied.

## KPIs

- Vendor response time
- Vendor completion time
- Vendor exception rate
- Rework rate
- Critical vendor coverage
- Vendor concentration risk
- Client-impacting vendor failures

## Principle

A strong vendor network makes MMC more valuable, but MMC's credibility depends on remaining the independent party coordinating and verifying—not the party quietly absorbing vendor responsibility.
