# Meridian Marine Co. — SOP & Training Architecture v1

## Objective

Create a training structure that allows MMC to transfer its operating standards to future operators without relying on informal founder memory.

## SOP hierarchy

### Level 1 — Principles

Define why MMC operates the way it does.

Examples:

- Independent oversight
- Client-first communication
- Evidence before conclusion
- Verify before closure
- Protect safety and confidentiality

### Level 2 — Core SOPs

Define the standard operating workflows.

Core areas:

- Client onboarding
- Vessel baseline
- Scheduled visit
- Reporting
- Finding/action management
- Client communication
- Incident escalation
- Vendor coordination
- Quality assurance
- Business continuity

### Level 3 — Task procedures

Explain specific repeatable tasks within the core SOPs.

Examples:

- Visit preparation
- Evidence capture
- Report assembly
- Vendor follow-up
- Record updates
- QA checks

### Level 4 — Checklists

Short execution aids used at the point of work.

## Training pathway

**ORIENTATION → SHADOW → SUPERVISED EXECUTION → QA REVIEW → INDEPENDENT EXECUTION → PERIODIC AUDIT**

## Operator orientation

Teach:

- MMC purpose and positioning
- Service boundaries
- Safety expectations
- Client communication standards
- Evidence standards
- Severity classification
- Escalation rules
- Confidentiality
- Vendor boundaries
- QA expectations

## Competency standard

An operator should demonstrate the ability to:

1. Prepare correctly.
2. Follow the approved visit scope.
3. Observe without overclaiming.
4. Capture useful evidence.
5. Identify and classify findings appropriately.
6. Escalate when required.
7. Communicate clearly.
8. Complete records accurately.
9. Follow through on assigned actions.
10. Recognize when a matter exceeds their authority or competence.

## Training evidence

Maintain a record of:

- Training completed
- SOPs reviewed
- Shadow visits
- Supervised visits
- QA outcomes
- Corrective coaching
- Competency sign-off
- Refresher requirements

## SOP change control

When an SOP changes:

**CHANGE → REVIEW → TRAIN/ACKNOWLEDGE → IMPLEMENT → QA → VERIFY**

Material changes should not silently enter field operations.

## Periodic refresh

Training should be refreshed when:

- SOPs materially change
- Repeated quality defects appear
- New service types are introduced
- New equipment/tools are adopted
- Incidents reveal a training gap
- An operator's performance indicates a need

## Founder knowledge transfer

The founder should continuously convert recurring decisions and lessons into documented SOPs rather than retaining them as unwritten knowledge.

## Principle

MMC becomes scalable when the service can be taught, executed, measured, and improved without depending on one person's memory.
