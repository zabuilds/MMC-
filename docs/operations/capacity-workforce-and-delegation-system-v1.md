# Meridian Marine Co. — Capacity, Workforce & Delegation System v1

## Purpose

Define how MMC measures workload, adds capacity, delegates work safely, and reduces founder dependency without allowing service quality to decline.

## Capacity model

Capacity must be measured against actual work, not simply vessel count.

Core factors include:

- Active vessels
- Visit frequency
- Visit complexity
- Travel/access requirements
- Finding/action volume
- Vendor coordination load
- Reporting load
- Incident volume
- QA workload
- Administrative workload

## Capacity states

Use a simple operating signal:

**GREEN → YELLOW → RED**

### Green

Capacity is comfortably supporting committed service.

### Yellow

Capacity is tightening and additional work requires deliberate scheduling or delegation.

### Red

Committed work is at risk or service quality is deteriorating. Growth should pause until capacity is restored.

## Workforce model

MMC capacity can be provided through appropriate combinations of:

- Founder/executive oversight
- Operations/coordinator support
- Authorized vessel operators
- QA/management capacity
- Approved specialist/vendor partners

Each role should have clear authority and boundaries.

## Delegation principle

Delegate **repeatable, documented, low-ambiguity work** before delegating high-risk judgment.

Founder attention should remain concentrated on:

**SALES + CLIENT TRUST + QA + EXCEPTIONS + STRATEGY**

## Delegation readiness test

A task is ready for delegation when:

- Scope is documented
- Required inputs are known
- Expected output is clear
- Escalation conditions are defined
- Operator has the required capability
- QA can verify the result
- Access is appropriately controlled

## Delegation ladder

**FOUNDER-LED → SUPERVISED → DELEGATED → QA-SAMPLED → FULLY ROUTINE**

High-risk or unusual work may move back down the ladder temporarily.

## Operator capacity

Monitor operator workload using:

- Assigned visits
- Travel burden
- Visit complexity
- Open findings/actions
- Reporting workload
- Incident involvement
- QA/rework requirements

Do not assume equal vessel counts represent equal workloads.

## Capacity planning

Before adding material volume, assess:

**DEMAND → REQUIRED WORK → AVAILABLE CAPACITY → GAP → CAPACITY ACTION → QUALITY CHECK**

Capacity actions may include:

- Better scheduling
- Delegation
- Additional operator capacity
- Vendor support
- Process simplification
- Temporary sales pacing

## Hiring/contracting trigger

Additional workforce capacity should be considered when:

- Utilization remains elevated
- Founder workload is consistently excessive
- Service commitments approach risk thresholds
- Growth opportunities are being declined because capacity is unavailable
- QA quality would otherwise be threatened

## Onboarding and training

New operators should progress through:

**ORIENTATION → SOP TRAINING → SHADOWING → SUPERVISED WORK → QA REVIEW → AUTHORIZED WORK**

Authorization should match demonstrated capability.

## Performance management

Review:

- Visit completion
- Reporting accuracy
- QA outcomes
- Escalation quality
- Communication
- Reliability
- Rework
- Client-impacting defects

Performance issues should result in coaching, retraining, increased supervision, reassignment, or other appropriate action.

## Founder dependency metric

Track the percentage of recurring operational work requiring direct founder involvement.

The target is progressive reduction while preserving quality and accountability.

## Growth stage alignment

### 1–5 vessels

Founder-led delivery and workload discovery.

### 6–10

Introduce repeatable delegation and structured support.

### 11–15

Build dependable operator capacity and reduce founder bottlenecks.

### 16–20

Develop capacity redundancy and management leverage.

### 21–25

Review organizational structure for the next scale stage.

## Capacity decision rule

Never wait for service failure before adding capacity.

When the system moves toward Yellow, management should identify the capacity constraint and act before it becomes Red.

## Principle

MMC should grow by increasing controlled capacity—not by asking the founder or operators to absorb unlimited workload.
